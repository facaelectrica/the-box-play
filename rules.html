3Breaks his fallWhen he loses balance, he turns toward the impact and catches himself with his hands, forearms or a knee.Not yet

Rule

  • Catch first. Brace against the surface he is about to hit.
  • Shield only when a catch is impossible: no time, no room, upside down, or arms blocked. Then his forearms go between his head and the surface.
  • His face never rests against a wall. Upside down, he tucks.

Status

Not yet. Head shielding works; the catch is in development, so for now he mostly shields.

4Human strength onlyEvery force he uses is one a person could produce. He pushes with his palms, never the backs of his hands.Partly

Rule

  • No external lifting.
  • Joint strength capped at human levels.
  • Grip no stronger than a sole or palm on wood.

Exception (agreed)

Get-ups replay recorded motion, so the poses are human but not muscle-driven. A firm push or a steep floor still knocks him down.

Status

  • Works: in get-ups, a hand on the floor turns palm-down and releases when lifted (V42).
  • Not yet: standing, his spine and knees sometimes exceed human strength.
  • Not yet: hands don't land flat on walls.

6BalancedHis weight stays over his support: his feet plus any hand braced on a wall. When it leaves that area, he steps, grabs or falls.Partly

Rule

  • A braced hand counts as support, like a foot.
  • He never holds a pose that couldn't balance.
  • Hands braced on the walls should keep him upright while the box is shaken side to side.

Status

  • Works: he braces on the wall he is tipping toward, not the far one (V42).
  • Not yet: 3 falls in 36 standing tests.
  • Not yet: no recovery step.
  • Not yet: no reaction to light pushes during a get-up.

How it's checked

Balance overlay (debug menu → balance) and a standing test set: tilts, shaking, pushes, off-centre stances.

Separate feature: breakable body

Switched on or off independently. Not ranked with the rules above. Works

  • Damage depends on impact speed: none below 2.8 m/s (4.2 m/s for hands and feet). Damage accumulates until a part breaks.
  • Feet and hands that are supporting him while standing never crack. Nothing counts during a get-up.
  • Wood sounds, branching cracks with chips, and dents in the box. Broken pieces keep moving and can split again.
  • If the head, neck or trunk breaks, he stops.
